# Residency, transfers, recipients, and processors

**RM-PRIVACY-TRANSFER-0001:** A transfer plan binds controller/business and processor/service-provider roles, sender/recipient/legal entity, tenant/account, data subjects/categories, purpose/actions, origin/destination regions, storage/transit/support/access locations, subprocessors, protection, retention, onward-transfer rules, and policy evidence.

**RM-PRIVACY-TRANSFER-0002:** Region identity distinguishes logical account region, physical storage/replica/backup, processing execution, support/administrator access, key custody, telemetry, failover, network transit, and recipient location; one region label cannot summarize all.

**RM-PRIVACY-TRANSFER-0003:** Recipient eligibility is revalidated at disclosure against identity, role, contract/policy generation, purpose, requested projection, device/application/channel, geography, expiry, and authority. Possession of a URL or object key is insufficient.

**RM-PRIVACY-TRANSFER-0004:** Processor instructions are immutable purpose/action/data/retention/recipient/security plans with controller authority, acceptance, versioning, audit/evidence obligations, subprocessor constraints, deletion/return, incident notification, and termination/recovery.

**RM-PRIVACY-TRANSFER-0005:** Subprocessor onboarding/change/revocation is a rollout over inventories, contracts/policy, data routes, keys, transfers, notices/objections where selected, existing copies, logs/backups, and deletion evidence; configuration change is not convergence.

**RM-PRIVACY-TRANSFER-0006:** Cross-region failover and disaster recovery cannot silently violate selected residency/transfer constraints. Emergency policy names scope, authority, duration, data, safeguards, audit, repatriation/deletion, and post-event review.

**RM-PRIVACY-TRANSFER-0007:** Recipient acknowledgment, service commit, replication, use, onward disclosure, return, deletion, and audit availability are separate milestones and cannot be inferred from successful transport.
